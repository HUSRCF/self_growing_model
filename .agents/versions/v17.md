## Scope and protocol

v17 changes two components while preserving the outer operation order:

1. Make derivative orders 2–4 materially affect the `q` partition rather than being almost eliminated by exponentially decaying weights.
2. Put MLP or HMM numerical material inside `F(q,r)` and adapt it by the already selected `(q,r)` pair.

The verified runtime order is `read_event -> sample_event -> event_drives_qprime -> execute_selected_rule`; HMM rule-memory sampling occurs inside the selected rule. It does not use a candidate rule to infer the event or next state.

Data and evaluation:

- 21 IBM double-pendulum videos, split by whole video into 13 train / 4 development / 4 test.
- Two angle channels, nominal fixed `dt=0.01 s`, and 32 causal observations at startup.
- No true velocities, physical parameters, future observations, or added observation channels.
- Development: 64 windows (16/video), 50 steps, 8 particles, seed 1729.
- Frozen test: the same count of 64 windows, 300 steps, 8 particles, seed 1729.
- The test videos had been examined in prior research, so this is a reused-data frozen confirmation, not a fresh blind test.
- One training seed; windows from the same videos are correlated, so reported summaries are descriptive rather than independent-replicate confidence estimates.

Protocol amendments matter. Amendment 01 replaced IQR scaling with train-set RMS-about-median scaling after initial high-order partitions became dominated by heavy-tailed third/fourth derivatives and noise-sensitive. Amendment 02 added matched controls, a no-pair-adaptation control, and fixed MLP/polynomial blends before test evaluation.

## Architecture and implementation

### Outer machine

`v17/runtime.py::LocalMachine` subclasses the v16 `Machine`. Rollout maintains the outer `q` register separately from history-diagnosed `q`. A discrepancy is saved as `contradiction`; the diagnosed state does not overwrite the event-selected register.

Numerical failures are absorbing: a non-finite prediction or a step exceeding 20 radians marks a particle failed, holds its last history, and retains its state.

### Selected high-order partition

For each angle, a causal derivative jet of orders 1–4 is centered by the training median and scaled by training RMS about that median. It is then weighted by `[1, 0.9, 0.8, 0.7]` and clustered into 8 states using a 17-point causal polynomial estimate.

The selected `rms17` partition had development centroid-separation shares of approximately 14.4%, 34.1%, 31.6%, and 19.9% for derivative orders 1–4, with a 0.604% state-flip rate under the package's synthetic 1 mrad perturbation diagnostic. The old scheme assigned only about 2.6% of centroid separation to order 4. This establishes that v17 changed the geometry; it does not establish that high-order emphasis improves test prediction.

### MLP local rule

The shared encoder is a previously trained two-hidden-layer, 128-unit-per-layer tanh MLP. v17 does not retrain it from scratch. The summary variant uses 20 causal features. Each of the 64 `(q,r)` pairs gets a ridge-regularized `129 x 2` correction head over `[1, standardized final hidden layer]`; development selected ridge 100.

The neural rule predicts a second difference and reconstructs the next angle as `2*y_t - y_(t-1) + acceleration_like_increment`. The frozen primary blends this local neural prediction 50/50 with the old polynomial prediction inside the already selected `(q,r)` rule.

### HMM local rule

The HMM route reuses an 8-state, order-3 ARHMM as shared initialization, then estimates `(q,r)`-conditioned hidden transitions and emissions using training posterior responsibilities. Its hidden state is local rule memory, not outer `q`. The adaptation is a posterior-weighted fit, not a jointly converged conditional EM procedure; startup filtering still uses the shared HMM.

Interpretation:

- The primary improved over original v16 E at 0.5/1/2 s by about 16.1%/11.4%/20.1%, but lost the advantage at 3 s.
- Old q plus the same 50/50 blend was better at 0.5 s than the primary (0.16072 versus 0.19074). The requested high-order q is therefore not the source of all improvement.
- High-order q alone regressed from the matched old-q polynomial's 0.22000 to 0.27235 at 0.5 s. The low-weight RMS/17 control also beat the high-order-only model on test.
- Pair adaptation is important: the shared MLP without pair heads scored 0.31534 at 0.5 s, versus 0.20282–0.21203 for pair-adapted MLPs and 0.16072–0.19074 for the blends.
- Pure local MLP has lower one-step conditional error than the blend, but the blend is more stable in recursive prediction. One-step fit is not a substitute for rollout evaluation.
- HMM performance is poor. At 3 s, failed-particle fraction is about 15.6% for old q and 17.0% for high-order q.

Median first-exit tracking duration at a 0.2 rad two-angle RMS threshold:

- Original v16 E: 0.555 s.
- Old q + polynomial: 0.575 s.
- Old q + 50/50 blend: 0.780 s.
- High-order q + 50/50 blend: 0.870 s.
- High-order q + local MLP: 0.915 s.
- High-order q + HMM: 0.170 s.

The primary's 0.870 s is about 57% longer than v16 E, but choosing a model from this table after viewing test would violate the freeze protocol.

## Remaining problems

- Register/history contradiction is not solved. The primary's first-0.5-s contradiction rate is about 24.62%, worse than v16 E's 17.28%.
- Uncertainty is under-dispersed. The primary's nominal 90% marginal interval covers only about 39.06% at 0.5 s; v16 E reaches about 80.08% but includes extra innovation, so attribution is confounded.
- High-order partitions alter boundaries and can conflict with histories generated by smooth local rules. Reachability and event closure remain open.
- No fresh blind test, multiple training seeds, calibrated probabilistic model, joint structure learning, or general mechanism recovery is established.
- Fixed clock only: the fitted interface rejects `dt != 0.01`.
